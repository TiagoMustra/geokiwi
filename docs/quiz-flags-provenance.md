# Flag question expansion

Checked on **2026-11-06**. The module adds **12 questions: eight `TEXT_MULTIPLE` sets and four `TEXT_SINGLE` facts**, all bilingual, revision 1. Answer labels, aliases and numeric IDs come from the existing `countries.json` records with `isCountry: true`.

## Scope and method

- The answer universe is the principal country catalogue, rather than all territories with a flag. In particular, Cook Islands and Niue have `isCountry: false` in this game and do not enter the Oceania answer set; this is a catalogue boundary, not a claim about their constitutional status.
- Continental questions follow `continente` in `countries.json` (`africa`, `europa`, `oceania`). Southeast Asia and South America follow the [UN Statistics Division M49 table](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/overview/), intersected with the same principal catalogue.
- All 54 African flags, all 46 European flags, all 14 Oceanian flags, all 11 Southeast Asian flags, all 12 South American flags and the other individual flags were rendered in labelled contact sheets and inspected. The 139 distinct relevant files in `public/flags` were also checked against `src/data/flag-svgs.json`: they match byte for byte.
- The bundled artwork identifies itself as [flag-icons](https://github.com/lipis/flag-icons), an SVG collection published by its maintainers. Its [4:3 collection](https://github.com/lipis/flag-icons/tree/main/flags/4x3) is a primary source for the exact visual data in the game, not an issuing authority for flag law; the questions do not depend on the collection’s standardised display proportions.
- Official national or intergovernmental descriptions are attached where available. Completeness was checked by scanning every flag in each bounded region, including the negative cases, rather than assuming that a few examples form a complete set.
- National flags count. Historical, presidential, military, naval, provincial and other subnational designs do not count. Afghanistan and Syria were deliberately avoided because recent competing or changed designs make generic prompts fragile.

## Complete grouped answer sets

| Question ID suffix | Complete ISO3 answer set | Why complete; important exclusions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `union-jack-oceania` | AUS, FJI, NZL, TUV | Of the 14 principal Oceanian countries, only these four national designs contain the Union Jack. Cook Islands and Niue are outside the principal catalogue; no other Oceanian flag depicts it. |
| `nordic-cross-europe` | DNK, FIN, ISL, NOR, SWE | The [Nordic Council of Ministers’ collection](https://www.norden.org/en/information/nordic-flags) identifies these five national Nordic-cross designs. The prompt requires the cross to reach all four edges and be shifted towards the hoist; the Greek canton, the centred British cross, and the short Swiss cross do not qualify. Faroe Islands and Åland are regional flags. |
| `vertical-tricolours-europe` | AND, BEL, FRA, IRL, ITA, MDA, ROU | These are the only European principal-country backgrounds composed of three vertical bands in three different colours. Coats of arms are explicitly ignored; equal band widths are deliberately **not** required because Andorra’s middle band is wider. Portugal, Malta and Vatican City have two background colours, while horizontal tricolours and fields with a triangle do not qualify. |
| `stars-southeast-asia` | MYS, MMR, PHL, SGP, TLS, VNM | The regional universe is BRN, KHM, IDN, LAO, MYS, MMR, PHL, SGP, THA, TLS, VNM. Of these, Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos and Thailand have no stars on their national designs; the six answers all have at least one, with the Philippine sun distinguished from its three stars. |
| `stars-south-america` | BRA, CHL, SUR, VEN | The universe is ARG, BOL, BRA, CHL, COL, ECU, GUY, PRY, PER, SUR, URY, VEN. The prompt expressly excludes stars inside coats of arms **or seals**, so neither Bolivia’s coat-of-arms stars nor Paraguay’s seal star counts; Argentine and Uruguayan suns do not count as stars. The remaining four excluded countries have no qualifying star. |
| `crescents-southeast-asia` | BRN, MYS, SGP | Only three of the 11 Southeast Asian national designs show a crescent. The prompt expressly includes a crescent contained in a coat of arms, so Brunei’s national emblem qualifies alongside the separate crescents in Malaysia and Singapore. |
| `weapons-africa` | AGO, KEN, MOZ, SWZ | All 54 African national flags were inspected. Angola shows a machete/catana, Kenya and Eswatini show spears, and Mozambique shows a rifle; the prompt names these objects, avoiding a disputed interpretation of whether Angola’s agricultural machete should symbolically be called a weapon. Uganda’s coat of arms has spears, but that coat of arms is **not on its national flag**, so Uganda is excluded. |
| `birds-africa` | EGY, UGA, ZMB, ZWE | All 54 national designs were inspected. Egypt’s coat-of-arms eagle, Uganda’s crested crane, Zambia’s eagle and the stylised Zimbabwe Bird qualify; isolated feathers on Eswatini’s flag do not depict a whole bird and are explicitly excluded by the prompt. |

The eight sets contain 3–7 answers each. Existing difficulty logic therefore requests one answer on Easy, half rounded up on Medium, and the complete set on Hard; the authored bank does not duplicate the same topic per difficulty.

## Official and publisher checks

- **Union Jack:** the [Australian PM&C flag booklet](https://www.pmc.gov.au/resources/australian-flags-booklet/part-1-australian-national-flag), [Fiji Foreign Affairs flag description](https://www.foreignaffairs.gov.fj/fiji-embassy-in-brussels-belgium/fiji-flag/), [New Zealand Ministry for Culture and Heritage](https://www.mch.govt.nz/our-work/flags-anthems-and-emblems/new-zealand-flag), and [Tuvalu National Flag Act, 2022 revision, Schedule 1](https://tuvalu-legislation.tv/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/1995/1995-0004/1995-0004_2.pdf) support the four qualifying designs.
- **Southeast Asian symbols:** [Singapore National Heritage Board](https://www.nhb.gov.sg/what-we-do/our-work/community-engagement/education/resources/national-symbols/national-flag), [Malaysian Foreign Affairs](https://www.kln.gov.my/web/usa_washington/history), and [Brunei Information Department, Brunei in Brief](https://www.information.gov.bn/Penerangan/Shared%20Documents/BIB2018_FINAL.pdf) describe the crescents; the latter is part of Brunei’s emblem. The [Philippine Constitution](https://officialgazette.gov.ph/constitutions/1987-constitution/), [Myanmar National Portal](https://myanmar.gov.mm/en/web/guest/national-symbols), [Timor-Leste Government](https://timor-leste.gov.tl/?p=34&lang=en), and [Vietnam Government](https://vietnam.gov.vn/default.aspx?catname=national-flag-emblem-anthem-declaration-of-independence-68960) describe the remaining star-bearing flags.
- **South American stars:** the [Brazilian Presidency](https://www.gov.br/planalto/pt-br/conheca-a-presidencia/biblioteca-da-pr/simbolos-nacionais/bandeira/bandeira-nacional) and [Chilean Government](https://www.gob.cl/nuestro-pais/) describe the flags; the [Surinamese Presidency](https://gov.sr/ministeries/kabinet-van-de-president/heritage-month-suriname/) expressly identifies the national flag’s golden star. The full accepted and excluded designs, including Venezuela’s eight stars and Paraguay’s seal, were checked against the bundled publisher SVGs.
- **African objects:** [Luanda Government](https://luanda.gov.ao/web/simbolos-nacionais) names Angola’s catana; the [Kenyan Government branding guide](https://trade.go.ke/sites/default/files/Guidelines/GoK%20Branding%20Guidelines.pdf) depicts the crossed spears; [Mozambican Foreign Affairs](https://minec.gov.mz/simbolos-nacionais/) names the firearm crossed with a hoe. [Eswatini’s government national-symbols page](https://gov.sz/index.php/national-symbols) was discoverable but slow to fetch; its two spears and isolated feathers were confirmed in the actual bundled SVG. No claim about the make/model of the Mozambican firearm is needed.
- **African birds:** [Egyptian Presidency](https://www.presidency.eg/EN/%D9%85%D8%B5%D8%B1/%D9%85%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%89/), [Ugandan Government](https://nam.go.ug/useful-information), [Zambia’s National Flag and Armorial Ensigns Act](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/National%20Flag%20and%20Armorial%20Ensigns%20Act.pdf), and [Zimbabwe Presidential Communications](https://deptcomms.org.zw/national-symbols/) support the four designs. The Zimbabwe figure deliberately counts as a stylised bird, rather than being mistaken for an illustration of a living animal.

## Individual facts and accuracy boundaries

| Golden frigatebird over sunrise and waves | KIR | [Kiribati National Identity Act 1989, Schedule 1](https://www.paclii.org/ki/legis/num_act/nia1989197.pdf) is the primary legislative text; its indexed excerpt describes the frigatebird and rising sun, while the full PDF fetch was access-blocked. The complete visual configuration was independently checked in the bundled publisher SVG; no species, ray-count or historical date claim is made. |

No existing Nepal, maple leaf, New Zealand four-star, Bhutan dragon, Union Flag cross-composition, Swiss square or Vatican keys clue is repeated here. The expansion supplies new grouped patterns and four different unique facts. It does not itself alter selection, suggestions, rendering or existing questions.

## Validation

All 12 entries pass the existing `validateQuestion`. Their IDs are distinct within the module and from the existing question bank, every answer belongs to an `isCountry` record, and every Portuguese and English label is accepted by `evaluateSubmission`; each multiple-answer set was also exercised to the Easy, Medium and Hard completion thresholds.
